Water & Other Things

A small collection of definitions, useful distinctions, and facts that might come in handy someday.

01 / Water terminology

Definition 001

What is a well?

A well is a hole or shaft dug, drilled, or bored into the ground to access groundwater. Water enters from surrounding soil or rock, often through a casing or screened section.

Wells may be used for drinking water, irrigation, industrial purposes, or monitoring groundwater.

Definition 002

What is a drinking water source?

A drinking water source is the natural supply or system from which water intended for human consumption originates. Examples include groundwater wells, springs, lakes, rivers, and reservoirs.

The source is not necessarily the tap: water may need treatment, storage, and distribution before it is safe to drink.

Definition 003

Groundwater vs. surface water

Groundwater is water stored beneath Earth's surface in the spaces and fractures of soil and rock. An aquifer is a geological formation that can store and transmit useful quantities of groundwater.

Surface water is found above ground, in places such as streams, lakes, and reservoirs.

Definition 004

What is a watershed?

A watershed is an area of land where rain and melting snow drain toward a shared outlet, such as a stream, lake, or river.

What happens on the land—fertilizer use, road runoff, construction, or spills—can affect water downstream.

02 / A quick distinction

Remember: A well is a way to access water. A drinking water source is where water for drinking comes from. A well can be a drinking water source, but not every drinking water source is a well.

03 / Random facts

  • 01. Pure water freezes at 0°C (32°F) and boils at 100°C (212°F) at standard atmospheric pressure.
  • 02. Water expands when it freezes, which is why ice is less dense than liquid water and floats.
  • 03. Groundwater can move very slowly through some aquifers—sometimes only inches or feet per day, though rates vary widely.
  • 04. The water cycle has no true starting point: evaporation, condensation, precipitation, infiltration, and runoff are connected processes.
  • 05. Most of Earth's water is salty. Only a small fraction is freshwater, and much of that freshwater is locked in glaciers and ice caps or stored underground.
  • 06. Sound travels faster in water than in air, because the physical properties of water transmit pressure waves more quickly.
  • 07. A clear glass of water is not proof that the water is safe. Some contaminants have no obvious taste, smell, or color.
  • 08. The word “salary” is often linked historically to the Latin word salarium; the popular claim that Roman soldiers were literally paid in salt is debated.
  • 09. Octopuses have three hearts: two pump blood through the gills, and one pumps it to the rest of the body.
  • 10. Bananas are berries in the botanical sense; strawberries are not considered true berries by that same definition.

04 / Practical note

If you rely on a private well, regular testing matters. The appropriate tests and schedule depend on local guidance and possible contaminants. In the United States, your state or local health department can explain how to test for bacteria, nitrate, and other locally relevant concerns.
